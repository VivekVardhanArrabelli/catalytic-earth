# Decisive orphan search — the honest result (2026-06-30)

**Goal:** find a target that isolates the atlas's *unique* capability — dark, with the
mechanism **not inferable from its own sequence/Pfam**, yet atlas-confident — so a lab could
confirm a mechanism no cheap tool predicts.

**Method:** dark proteins from Pfam **clan sibling** families of the 4 atlas families (same fold,
different family) → keep <25% identity to atlas → fold + foldseek → keep TM≥0.70 → inspect each
candidate's **own** Pfam.

**Funnel:** 676 sibling-family dark candidates → 12 sequence-orphans (<25%) → **4 atlas-confident**.

**Result:** all 4 confident orphans carry their **own** family annotation that already reveals the
mechanism — PF03098 (animal heme peroxidase), PF08192 (peptidase S64), PF00282 (PLP decarboxylase),
PF03761 ("trypsin-6-like" DUF). **No target was found where the mechanism is hidden from sequence but
revealed by the atlas.**

**Why (mechanistic, not a fluke):** the atlas's 4 mechanisms live in large, ancient,
**sequence-recognisable** superfamilies. Sequence/Pfam always recovers at least the mechanism class —
even for proteins that are sequence-orphans to the atlas's *specific* members. So for these families
the atlas is **never the only path** to the mechanism.

**Implication:** the atlas's *unique* claim ("mechanism where sequence is blind") is **not
demonstrable for its current families** — they are sequence-saturated. Its **proven** value stands:
better twilight-zone accuracy, fail-safe abstention, mechanism-searchability. The unique capability
would only show up in mechanism classes that are **not** sequence-coherent (convergent active sites,
novel folds) — which means **broadening the atlas**, not running a lab experiment now.
